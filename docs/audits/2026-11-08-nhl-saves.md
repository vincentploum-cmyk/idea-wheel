# What predicts a goalie's saves (2026-11-08)

Vincent, 2026-11-08: *can we look at the NHL goal saves props? We need to find something to predict saves.*

Measured on every regular-season start of 2024-25 (2,624), 2025-26 (2,624) and 2026-27 to November 7 (536): the public
box scores through `tools/predictors/fetch.mjs`, one row per starting goalie built by `tools/predictors/saves_rows.py`,
every read from games dated before the start. Formulas are fitted on 2024-25 and scored unchanged on 2025-26 and on
this season. The script is `tools/predictors/saves.py`; every table quoted is in
[2026-11-08-nhl-saves-tables.md](2026-11-08-nhl-saves-tables.md).

## In one paragraph

**Saves are shots against.** A starter's saves track the shots he faced at r = 0.97; his save percentage that night
moves them by about a save. So the question is what predicts the shots, and the answer is **both teams**: the
opponent's shots a game and the goalie's own team's shots allowed a game, each blended with last season. Together they
rank the night at ρ 0.25, against 0.20 for the opponent alone, 0.17 for his team alone, and 0.12 for **his own saves a
start, the figure a props screen shows** (the weakest volume read). His save percentage predicts nothing (ρ 0.04); the
league's .900 does as well as his own. Small extras: home goalies make 0.7 to 0.9 fewer saves, and some rinks credit
more shots (Colorado +4%, Dallas −8%). Rest, back to backs, penalties, the goal difference, his pull rate and the last-5
windows add nothing. Even the best formula explains only 8% of a night (the spread around it is 6.8 saves), so the
**edge is against a line set near his own average**: when the matchup put him 2+ saves over his own average, the over
landed 66 to 78% of the time; 2+ under, 30 to 34%. On the site, the Saves table projects about **one save too many**
and says its chances with **too narrow a spread** (SD 5.4 against 6.8): it said 25+ at 55% when 47% landed.

## 1. The night

| | 2024-25 | 2025-26 | 2026-27 |
|---|---|---|---|
| saves a start | 24.7 | 24.2 | 23.7 |
| SD | 7.0 | 7.1 | 6.8 |
| 25+ / 28+ / 30+ | 50 / 33 / 23% | 47 / 31 / 22% | 44 / 26 / 19% |
| pulled before 50' (regulation) | 4.3% | 4.8% | 5.2% |

A pulled start averages 11 saves; overtime adds 1.7. The starter faces 97% of the shots his team allows, because
relief goalies take the rest. The league is shooting less this season (27.4 shots a team against 28.3 two seasons
ago), so 30 saves is a one-in-five night.

The season figure beats every short window, the way it did for skaters' shots. Attempts and chances say no more than
shots on goal.

Shots first, then the save percentage: the league's .900 scores 5.31 / 5.04 MAE, his shrunk save % (the site's)
5.31 / 5.05, his raw 5.32 / 5.08. The goalie is not the read; the shots are.

Refitted on both seasons, the saves are about `0.95 + 0.84·league + 0.76·(opponent's shots − league) + 0.69·(team's shots
allowed − league) − 0.9 at home`, with each team's shots blended into last season at games / (games + 10).

## 4. The spread

The miss around the fitted projection has an SD of 6.8 in every band (6.1 on starts he finished; the pulled starts make
the left tail). A normal at 6.8 is calibrated (25+: said 46%, landed 47%; 28+: said 30%, landed 31%), and so are the fitted
residuals pooled over every mark from 20 to 32: a 50–60% call landed 57%, a 60–70% call 66%, a 70–80% call 76%. The site's normal at 6 × save % (5.4) is too narrow, and with the shots it reads it
runs about one save high: it said 22+ at 74% (64% landed) and 25+ at 55% (47% landed).

## 5. Against a line near his own average

The book's saves lines are on record only from the 2026-10-31 pull, one file a day and not kept. The stand-in here is
his saves a start this season rounded to the half below (o24.5 for 24.9). By how far the formula sat from it (2025-26
and this season, 2,687 starts):

| formula − line | starts | over landed |
|---|---|---|
| −3 or less | 283 | 30% |
| −3 to −2 | 277 | 34% |
| −2 to −1 | 404 | 43% |
| −1 to 0 | 478 | 48% |
| 0 to +1 | 467 | 54% |
| +1 to +2 | 336 | 60% |
| +2 to +3 | 224 | 66% |
| +3 or more | 218 | 78% |

With goalies 20+ starts in (his average steadier), the ends are 63% over at +2 and 33% at −2. A book prices
better than a raw average, so the edge against real lines will be smaller. The pattern holds, though: an average
collected against easy schedules reads high, and one collected against hard schedules reads low. The over at his
own average landed 50.4%, so the pulled starts do not tilt every line to the under.

## 6. The rinks

The two sides' shots at a rink, against the same teams' totals on the road (last season and this, shrunk at 20 home
games). Highest: Colorado 1.041, Minnesota 1.030, Toronto 1.022, Buffalo 1.020, Calgary 1.018. Lowest: Dallas 0.924,
Philadelphia 0.936, Chicago 0.944, the Rangers 0.955, Carolina 0.965. It is worth half a save at the extremes; a home
team's own style at home is in it too.

## What it means for the site (not shipped; Vincent's call)

The Saves table (`savesRow` in `lib/nhl-data/goalies-pure.js`, shots from `picks.js`) reads the board's λ shots summed
over the opponent's lineup × his shrunk save %, with a normal at 6 × save %. The study suggests:

1. **The spread**: 6.8 saves, not 5.4. This is the largest error in the chances it states.
2. **The starter's share**: × 0.97 of the shots the opponent is expected to take.
3. **The team's side**: blend his team's shots allowed in (about 0.7 of its gap to the league, beside the opponent's
   0.76), and take 0.45 off at home and add 0.45 away. The board's λ already reads the defending team's table by
   position, so part of this may be in it. The stand-in above is the opponent's team shots, not the board's sum, which
   needs the signed-in scorecard to grade.
4. **The save %**: his own adds nothing measurable. It can stay as the shrunk figure; it is harmless.
5. **Keep the book's lines a day** (`data/propfinder/goalie-props.json` is overwritten every pull), so the over's edge
   can be graded against real prices rather than the stand-in.
6. `SAVES_LEAD` = 30 is a one-in-five night this season; the mark-only fallback almost never makes a play.

## Reproduce

```bash
for y in 2024:2024-10-04:2025-04-17 2025:2025-10-07:2026-04-16 2026:2026-10-01:$(date -u +%F); do IFS=: read s a b <<< "$y"; node tools/predictors/fetch.mjs $a $b .nhl-data/predictors/$s/games; done
python3 -I tools/predictors/saves_rows.py .nhl-data/predictors/saves/rows.csv .nhl-data/predictors/{2024,2025,2026}/games
python3 -I tools/predictors/saves.py .nhl-data/predictors/saves/rows.csv --out .nhl-data/predictors/saves/report.md
```
