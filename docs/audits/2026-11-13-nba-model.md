# The NBA: a learned read blended into the board

Vincent, 2026-11-13, after the stars audit of the same day found no setting to tune: "so why not putting in some REAL
effort to improve our accuracy". The question changed from "which knob" to "how much accuracy is left in the data, and
can the board have it".

## How much is left

`tools/audit/nba_model.py`: gradient-boosted trees per stat on ~130 things the box scores know before a game (79,014
player-games, four seasons), trained on the seasons before and scored on the next, beside the site's board on the same
player-games (tables in `2026-11-13-nba-model-tables.md`).

| Average miss | 2025-26: the board | the model | half and half | 2026-27: the board | the model | half and half |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Points | 4.862 | 4.777 | **4.773** | 4.774 | 4.724 | **4.706** |
| Rebounds | 1.955 | 1.945 | **1.935** | 1.900 | 1.906 | **1.886** |
| Assists | 1.441 | 1.432 | **1.427** | 1.408 | 1.410 | **1.398** |
| Threes | 1.034 | 1.033 | **1.029** | 1.009 | 1.004 | **1.002** |

So the box scores hold about 2% more on points and under 1% on the rest, and the board and the model see different
things: together they beat either. Each group of features dropped in turn (the model's points miss over 2025-26, 4.777
whole): without **who is out and who is back among his teammates** 4.842; without **tonight's start and his minutes in
that role** 4.806; without the opponent 4.791; without pace, the book's spread and total, rest and home 4.774–4.776 —
nothing. The same order this season.

Where it parts from the board is where the stars audit found the board off: 20+ scorers with a teammate out (the board
+0.98 high, the model −0.03), with nobody out (−1.06, −0.38), where the team ceiling cut 8%+ (−1.90, −0.31). The model
reads an absence and a **return** the same way (a teammate back after missing most of a player's recent games takes
production away; the board had only the team ceiling for that).

## Built into the board (`lib/nba/model.js`, the cocktail's `model`)

- **The features** (`modelFeatures`, 110): his production a minute at four memories (half-lives 3, 8, 20, 50 games) for
  twelve box-score figures, his minutes, his last three / ten / season with their spread and best; tonight's role (in the
  five or not, his minutes in that role this season); the teammates: for each one his share of the player's last twenty
  games against whether he plays tonight, × what he produces — the absences (`out_*`) and the returns (`back_*`) apart.
  No opponent, pace or book in it: they added nothing, and the board's own matchup stays on the figure.
- **One code path**: the replay writes the training file with the site's own function (`nba-audit.mjs --features`), so
  the site reads exactly what the trees were trained on. `tools/audit/nba_model_fit.py` fits LightGBM (Poisson, 350 trees
  of 15 leaves a stat) and writes `lib/nba/model-trees.json` (0.9 MB, loaded by `lib/nba/store.js`, server side only).
- **The blend**: the board's projection and the model's, half each (`modelFactor`, held to ×0.6–1.6), as a factor on the
  ladder's environment — so the centre, the floor and ceiling, every rung, the shuffle and the lists move together. PRA
  moves by its parts. Steals and blocks are as before. The card's hover names both figures.

trained on all four and this season through Nov 12 (58,948 player-nights).

## What this is not

- Not a test against the book. The replay has no real lines; whether the board beats a price is the site's own graded
  nights (the next audit, on the store).
- Not self-updating: the trees are a file. Refit monthly, or when a season starts (the README's commands; about 25 minutes
  of replays).
- The calibration's fit was learned on the board before the blend; it relearns with the next grading
  (`POST /api/nba/refresh?only=calibration`).
