# The by-line read, checked (2026-10-31)

Vincent, 2026-10-31, of the slot badges the rink gained that afternoon (LW1 · C2 · D2, each tinted by the
opponent's rank against that slot, the rink head naming the opponent's softest line): *Can we do a check on
the games in the past to see if the new line identifiers help? In other words, was it indeed true that the LW1
had the most shots in that game tonight?*

Every skater-game of 2025-26 (1,312 games, 47,231 skater-games) and of 2026-27 to October 30 (162 games) was
replayed against the opponent's by-slot table as it stood that morning — what it gives up per skater-game to
the LW1, the C2, the D1 … this season's games alone, ranked over the league with 1 = allows the most, a cell
from three skater-games, the softest slot the best rank (the Defense tab's by-line table, `defenseBySlot` and
`slotBoard`) — and graded on the box score. The script is `tools/audit/nhl_lines.py`; every table is in the
appendix, [2026-10-31-nhl-lines-tables.md](2026-10-31-nhl-lines-tables.md). The public box score carries no
line slots, so here a skater's slot is his ice-time rank inside his position that night (the LW with the most
minutes is LW1; the centres are the forwards who took the draws, `tools/predictors/relabel.py`): the lines as
they were played rather than as the beat writers posted them. Nothing on the site changed.

## In one paragraph

**No.** The skater at the slot the opponent ranked softest against was his team's top shooter in 9.4% of
2,390 team-games last season (9.8% of 163 this season) — the same as that slot's base rate (9.6% / 9.9%),
because the softest slot the ranking names is mostly a third- or fourth-line cell (LW3, RW4, LW4, C4 lead the
list) where a handful of skater-games swings the rank. The skater with the highest own shot rate going in was
the top shooter 32.5% of the time. Across every skater-game the slot table does sort a little (the soft third
+2.8% shots over his own rate, the tough third −4.9%) but the position table the ice already carries sorts
more (+3.7% / −7.3%), and on top of his own rate and the position cell the slot cell carries a coefficient of
−0.01 (z −0.3) for shots and 0.03 (z 0.8) for goals: nothing of its own. A team's slot cells barely persist
(odd games against even games 0.21 last season, 0.01 this season, against 0.48 and 0.16 for the position
cells). The by-line read is colour, and the "softest line" in the rink head is noise.

## What to do with it

- The slot badge's tint and the rink head's "softest line" should not drive a pick. The position table (the
  ice's bands, the chips' environment, the study's λ) carries what the opponent gives up; the slot table is a
  noisier cut of the same thing.
- If the by-line view stays on the rink, it is colour, like the flame and the snowflake, and the head's
  "softest line" would be more honest as "the slot where the opponent ranks lowest against the league" with
  the skater-games behind the cell — or the head line could go and the badges stay as the slot alone.
- A real by-line signal would need the lines as deployed, not as posted, and a season of them; the site's rows
  carry the posted lines, which this replay could not read (the store is not reachable from the audit).

## Blind spots

- The slots here are ice-time ranks inside the position, not the posted lines. On the site LW1 is the beat
  writer's first-line left wing; here he is the left wing who played the most. The two agree most nights; where
  they differ, the ice-time rank is the line that was played.
- The 2026-27 check is 163 team-games and 2,786 skater-games: the lifts there carry ±3 points of noise.
