# The stat lists' top 10: where the misses come from (2026-11-24)

Vincent, 2026-11-24: "In the points, assists, rebounds and threes views, we need to see how we can reduce the number of
misses in the top 10. See if any patterns can be detected to increase the accuracy for each one of them."

**What is graded.** The Points / Rebounds / Assists / Threes tabs (`NbaStatList` → `tierRows`) list the night by projection;
each row shows the highest round mark he clears at 70% (strong), 55% (lean) and 40% (thin) on the shuffle's chance
(`tierBoard`). A miss is a mark shown in red once the game is in. Here: the night's ten highest projections per stat, every
mark they show.

**Data.** Three reads of the same top 10. (1) **The site's own boards**: a copy of the store (`nba/<date>/` for Oct 21 –
Nov 23: the stored PropFinder pull, schedule, RotoWire lineups, injuries and box scores; `tools/audit/nba-store-dump.mjs`)
rebuilt with the site's `boardFor` / `shuffleFor` (`tools/audit/nba-store-top10.mjs --calibrate`, the calibration learned
before each night put in place first, as the refresh would have had it): 33 nights, 3,436 marks graded, 29 on a player who
did not play left out, 5 of 253 games left out because they were over before the stored pull was taken. (2) The replay
(`npm run audit:nba … --marks-all`: `buildBoard` on a pull rebuilt from ESPN's logs, pseudo lines, the box score's starters
for RotoWire) over 2025-26 (160 nights, the learned trees off since they were fitted on that season), 16,959 marks. (3) The
same replay over this season (30 nights, trees on), as a check on (1). The back-to-back from the schedules (his team played
the night before). Script: `tools/audit/nba_top10.py`; tables: [2026-11-24-nba-top10-tables.md](2026-11-24-nba-top10-tables.md)
(`site` = the store's boards).

## The answer: most misses are the chance playing out; one pattern holds, the back-to-back

- **The marks land at the chance they state, or better.** The site's boards: strong said 76–79%, hit 79–84%; lean said
  61–64%, hit 60–68%; thin said 46–48%, hit 43–52%. Points and rebounds land 3–7 points above what they say, assists and
  threes within 3. 2025-26 replayed: every stat and tier within 3 points. A 70% mark misses 3 nights in 10 by design.
- **A night's misses in the top 10, on the site's boards**: points 7.1, threes 8.2, rebounds 9.7, assists 11.1 (2025-26:
  7.9, 8.9, 11.3, 10.7). In 2025-26: strong 2.0–2.4 a night, lean 2.4–3.8, thin 3.4–5.6: **the thin column is half of them** (a 40–50%
  mark).
- **Half the misses come with lost minutes** (the replays). He played under 90% of the minutes read on 52% (2025-26) and 50% (this season)
  of the missed marks; under 75% of the read, the marks hit 32–36%. The minutes read for a regular (28+ minutes) runs 2.2–2.4
  minutes high in both seasons, 3.4–3.6 for a 35–38 minute read and 3.1–3.2 when tonight's read sits 2+ above his last ten;
  his rate a minute makes up for it (the projection carries no bias), so this is where the spread lives, not a bias to take
  out. Games with a spread of 12+: 47% / 38% of the regulars lose 4+ minutes, against 31% / 29% in close games.

## Per stat: what separates the misses, beyond the chance stated

One read at a time over the stated chance (hit = σ(a + b·logit(said) + c·z(read))). A read is called a pattern only with
the same sign and weight on the site's boards and in 2025-26.

| Stat | Second night of a back-to-back (site's boards: strong marks hit, b2b against the rest; every tier, hit − said) | 2025-26 replayed | Otherwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assists | **74% against 80%**; −4.8 against +0.9 | 69% against 80% (n 270); −5.5 against +3.4 | the highest minutes reads (37+) land 58% said 62% this season |
| Rebounds | **70% against 81%**; −2.0 against +6.5 | 73% against 77%; −1.2 against +2.7 | the highest minutes reads 55% said 61% this season, −2.0 in 2025-26 |
| Points | **80% against 85%**; −1.2 against +6.3 | 79% against 80%; −1.6 against +1.0 | big underdog (+6.5): −11.4 in this season's replay, −1.4 in 2025-26 |
| Threes | none: 80% either way; +1.5 against +0.2 | 76% against 77% | nothing holds |

So on the site's boards a back-to-back costs points, rebounds and assists 6–9 points of hit rate against the other nights;
2025-26 agrees on assists and rebounds and barely on points. This season only (the site's boards and the replay agree,
2025-26 does not): a mark close to the projection (rebounds, assists, threes) lands less often than said and a mark well
under it more often, i.e. the chance curve reads too flat this season.

Leaving the rest out on purpose: his sd on the stat, his share of his last ten at the mark, the game total, the
environment and team-scale factors, place on the list and games played each moved one season and not the other; folded into
a calibration with the back-to-back they made this season's Brier worse.

**Why the board missed the back-to-back.** The cocktail tested rest on every player and found nothing (`lib/nba/picks.js`):
pooled, it cancels. Actual over projected, by the third of the night's projections (2025-26 / this season):

| Stat | top third, rested | top third, 2nd night | bottom third, rested | bottom third, 2nd night |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Assists | 1.032 / 0.988 | 0.982 / 0.972 | 1.061 / 0.949 | 1.116 / 1.113 |
| Rebounds | 0.997 / 0.986 | 0.965 / 0.971 | 1.019 / 0.992 | 1.073 / 0.998 |
| Points | 1.003 / 0.991 | 0.984 / 0.965 | 1.001 / 0.926 | 1.064 / 1.068 |
| Threes | 0.978 / 1.004 | 0.986 / 0.971 | 1.048 / 0.981 | 1.100 / 1.072 |

The regulars give 2–5% back on the second night and the bench takes it. Their minutes on that night fall about a minute
further under the read (regulars 28+: 2.8 against 2.3 minutes short in 2025-26, 2.7 against 2.1 this season).

**The back-to-back as a shift on the chance** (logit p + c on the second night, c fitted on 2025-26, read on the site's
boards, the tiers re-read):

| Stat | odds × | Brier (every height) | misses / marks shown | 2nd-night marks hit |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Assists | 0.82 | 0.11166 → 0.11161 | 369 / 953 → 358 / 953 | 56.4% → 63.2% |
| Rebounds | 0.95 | 0.11001 → 0.10995 | 339 / 955 → 336 / 954 | 56.8% → 58.3% |
| Points | 0.95 | 0.11478 → 0.11478 | 241 / 777 → 236 / 770 | 62.8% → 64.3% |
| Threes | 0.91 | 0.12568 → 0.12582 | 274 / 780 → 272 / 782 | 66.4% → 68.2% |

Assists is the one worth a change: 11 fewer misses over 33 nights, its second-night rows 56% → 63%. On the site's boards
points and rebounds would take a bigger shift than 2025-26 fitted (their gap is 7.5 and 8.5 points there), with only 33
nights behind it.

**The back-to-back flag was wrong.** The board's `b2b` (the badges, and this audit's tag) compared a player's newest log date
to the day before the slate. PropFinder's `gameDate` is a UTC timestamp and the pull kept its first ten characters, so an
evening ET game carries the next day: the flag fired on the team that had played **two** nights ago (2,438 of its 2,652
player-night flags, Oct 21 – Nov 23) and almost never on a real back-to-back (206 of 1,972). Fixed 2026-11-24: the pull keeps
the ET night as `day` (`etDayOf` in `lib/picks/propfinder.js`), the flag reads it (`playedLastNight` in `lib/picks/board.js`);
a pull stored before has only the UTC date, read as last night when it is the slate's own date and not tonight's opponent
(582 of the real back-to-backs caught, none wrongly). The scorecard's `asOf` cut reads `day` too: it had dropped the night
before from the replayed boards' logs (dated the slate in UTC), so the graded board was not quite the live one.
