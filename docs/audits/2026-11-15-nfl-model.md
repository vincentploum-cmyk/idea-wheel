# The NFL: a learned read blended into the board (2026-11-15)

Vincent, 2026-11-15: "build us the best nfl prediction model for tds, rushing yards, receiving yards and passing yards".
The NBA's answer of 2026-11-13 (boosted trees beside the board, half each) asked of the NFL: how much more do PropFinder's
logs hold than the board reads, and can the board have it.

**Shipped**: trees for receiving yards, rushing yards, receptions and the anytime touchdown, blended half and half into the
board's read (`lib/nfl/model.js`, `lib/nfl/model-trees.json`, `PARAMS.MODEL`). **Not shipped**: passing yards (the blend cost
yards on the forward blocks).

## The data

- PropFinder's logs from the site's stored pulls (the `nfl-pf-export` artifact of 2026-11-15: w06–w10), every skill player
  back to 2024, a rostered week of zeros included; ESPN's schedule and lines for 2024, 2025 and 2026's weeks 1–10
  (`nfl-fetch.mjs`).
- The site's board replayed week by week on the logs cut before the week (`nfl-pf-audit.mjs --features`): 2024's weeks
  6–18, 2025's 1–18, 2026's 1–10 — **12,552 player-games**, each with the features as `lib/nfl/model.js` computes them (the
  site's own function), the board's read of every market and what he got.

## The features (`modelFeatures`, 170 read by the trees)

From his own log before the game, his active games (a week of zeros is a game he was not in):
- every box-score figure, his target and carry shares, red-zone looks, air yards and yards after the catch as a game's
  average at four memories (half-lives 2, 5, 12 and 30 games);
- what he makes of a look at each memory (yards a target, a carry, an attempt; his catch rate), and his team's targets and
  carries those games (his own count ÷ his share);
- his last three, best and worst of late, spread, season and last game for the main figures;
- how often he has been in the game lately and the days since his last one;
- tonight: home, his team's implied points, the total, his depth slot;
- his side: its players' five-game memories summed (a man without a game in 24 days left off), his part of them, the
  starting quarterback's passing (`teamFigures`).

The board's own reads are written to the training file (`b_*`) and read by no tree: with them the trees did no better
(2026: receiving 21.51 either way), and the trees stay free of the board's code.

## What the trees add (`tools/audit/nfl_model.py`)

LightGBM, Poisson for the yards (300 trees of 7 leaves) then a line by least absolute deviation on the training weeks'
out-of-fold answers (the board's figure is the game he is likeliest to have; the trees' mean runs 5 yards above it), binary
for the touchdown (250 trees). Scored on the board's lists (receivers and backs projected 10+ yards, 1+ receptions; every
skill player's touchdown chance), same player-games, paired bootstrap.

Season by season (2024 → 2025, 2024–25 → 2026, and 2025–26 → 2024 as a third look) the same: receiving −0.13, −0.45, −0.07;
rushing −0.38, −0.63, −0.08; the touchdown Brier −0.0007, −0.0014, −0.0004. The gain grows with the training rows.

- **Passing is left alone.** 400–1,200 starts to learn from; the blend lost on 2025 (+0.85, +1.76), won on 2026 (−0.44,
  −1.78), +0.50 pooled. A passing-only feature set, smaller trees and a quarter weight moved nothing. To be asked again when
  2026 is whole (the trend with more starts is the right way).
- **No group of features carries it.** Each left out in turn (the side's figures, tonight, availability, the shares, the red
  zone and air yards, efficiency, the windows): the pooled gain stays within 0.04 yards of whole.
- **The regulars missing tonight add next to nothing**, even read off the game itself (more than the site knows before
  kickoff): rushing 0.05–0.10 better on two seasons and level on the third, receiving 0.06 worse on one and level on two.
  The volume study's answer (2026-11-03) again; not built.
- **Settings**: 4, 7 or 15 leaves, 150–600 trees, more or less regularisation: within 0.05 of each other. Without the LAD
  line the trees' mean costs 0.2–0.4 yards of miss.

## Built

- `lib/nfl/model.js` (pure): `modelFeatures`, `teamFigures`, `modelRead` (a market only at the positions it was fitted for),
  `modelBlend` (yards half and half; a chance on its log-odds), `modelText` (the hover). The tree walker is the NBA's.
- `lib/nfl/projections.js`: each market's tested read first, then the features, then the blend, then PropFinder's pages, the
  floor / ceiling and the rungs on the blended figure; the touchdown chance blended after the engine, before the pages, so
  the first half, 2+ and first touchdown follow. `model: { pred, before }` on every blended read.
- `lib/nfl/build.js`: the site's build with `lib/nfl/model-trees.json` (0.35 MB, server side; the payload names the trees and
  does not carry them). `lib/nfl/picks.js` stays pure: its own `buildBoard` reads no trees.
- `tools/audit/nfl_model_fit.py` writes the trees and the parity fixture; `__tests__/nfl-model.test.js` (LightGBM's own
  answers on ten player-games, the features, the blend on a board).

## Not found here

- PropFinder's pages added this morning (the lines, funnels, tendencies, alignment, coverage players, first-drive
  tables, the edges board) are one snapshot each: nothing to train or grade on until weeks of them are stored.
- The ceiling is still volume (2026-11-03: knowing his targets would lift receiving's close calls from 28% to 43%). The
  trees read his recent volume better than the board's averages; they do not know tonight's.

## Across the markets: a quarterback and his receivers (the same day)

Vincent: "look at the predictions across each type also … if Trevor Lawrence is projected to throw 300 yards, we need to see
who within his team gets the lion share of the receiving yards". On the replay with the trees (1,058 team-games with a
starter's read): the side's receivers summed read 194 yards against the quarterback's 225 (each projection is the game that
man is likeliest to have; the median ratio 0.87, its spread 0.16), and the two reads agree only loosely (correlation 0.41).

- **The quarterback off his receivers**: their sum alone 61.42 against the passing read's 60.37 on 2026; both together 59.84
  (−0.52, −1.35 to +0.31), and no better fitted the other way round (59.91 against 59.75). Not built.
- **The receivers pulled toward their quarterback** (each × 1 + a · (Q / (ratio · S) − 1), within ×0.8–1.25, the ratio from the
  seasons before): 2025 receiving 20.75 → 20.55 at a = 0.35 (−0.20, −0.29 to −0.12); 2026 22.24 → 22.13 (−0.11, −0.24 to
  +0.01); counting only the men with a game in 24 days −0.16 and −0.08 (−0.22 to +0.05). Receptions gained nothing on 2026 and
  lost two points of close calls. The ratio is 1.19 / 1.14 / 1.26 by season and 1.11 on the live roster of week 10. Not built:
  too small and too unsteady to move every receiver by.
- **Built: the view.** Each receiver's part of his side's projected receiving yards (`teamShare`) and the largest five on the
  quarterback's passing read (`to`), a "Goes to" line under every quarterback and on his card.

Also fixed the same day: the trees' `home` read 0 for every player (the side's venue is 'H' / 'A'); the training file and the
trees were built again with it (2026 receiving 21.62, rushing 20.37, the Brier 0.1270: the same to 0.03).

## A teammate back (the same day)

Vincent, on week 10's Jets: "Why would the second rb get more rushing yards projected?" — Braelon Allen (RB2) 55 over Breece
Hall (RB1) 46. Hall missed six games (Sept 27 to Nov 8); Allen carried 14–24 times in them (59–147 yards), and in Hall's first
game back 8 times for 33 to Hall's 15 for 61. The board reads Allen's season (66 a game) and Hall's (56), then Buffalo's
table by slot (×1.075 at RB2, ×0.924 at RB1): 63 against 47. The trees read the last game harder and narrow it (59 against
53); the blend still has Allen ahead.

Tested as the NBA's `back_*`: for each teammate in tonight's game, the part of the player's last five games he missed × his
share of the carries / targets (all teammates, and those at his position). Added to the trees on the four forward blocks:
receiving −0.013 (−0.036 to +0.010), rushing +0.018 (−0.028 to +0.062). In the 260 rushing games with a same-position regular
back the board does not over-read (it is 1.6 yards under); those games are just harder (a miss of 23.3 against 20.8). Not
built: one case the read gets wrong, no pattern to fit.
