# The NBA: is the board too conservative with the big players?

Vincent, 2026-11-13: "Our nba model is still too conservative in its predictions. Especially with the big players in
favorable matchups as well as injury impacts. Let's investigate all the angles we can use to increase our accuracy … for
example what are the propfinder and player thresholds that need to happen to predict a particular player will have 3
threes."

Two studies, both on ESPN's box scores:

- **The board against what landed** (`tools/audit/nba_stars.py` on the site's board replayed, `nba-audit.mjs --cocktail
  site --marks`): 2025-26 whole (18,800 player-nights a stat) and this season through Nov 12 (3,050). Tables in
  `2026-11-13-nba-stars-tables.md`.
- **What it takes** (`tools/audit/nba_what_it_takes.py`): 71,456 player-games of four seasons, the thresholds searched on
  2024-25 and read unchanged on 2025-26 and this season. Tables in `2026-11-13-nba-what-it-takes-tables.md`.

## The short answer

**The board's centre is not low, for anyone.** A player projected 20+ points got 24.13 on a 24.18 projection over 2025-26
and 23.20 on 23.39 this season. In the two spots named, it runs **high**, not low:

| Projected 20+ points | 2025-26: projected → landed | this season |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| all of them | 24.18 → 24.13 | 23.39 → 23.20 |
| opponent in the soft third | 24.61 → 23.77 (**+0.84 high**) | 24.23 → 23.70 (+0.53 high) |
| opponent top five | 24.75 → 23.73 (+1.01 high) | 24.48 → 23.17 (+1.31 high) |
| a teammate out (lifted 3%+) | 24.70 → 23.72 (+0.98 high) | 24.10 → 23.34 (+0.76 high) |
| opponent in the tough third | 23.61 → 24.51 (0.90 low) | 22.44 → 22.55 |
| nobody out | 23.51 → 24.54 (1.03 low) | 23.18 → 23.27 |

The same holds for the chances. A star's 30+ points: said 30.2%, landed 30.5% over 2025-26; against a soft-third defense
34.6% / 34.3%; with a teammate out 36.1% / 33.8%; soft and a teammate out 40.7% / 41.0%. 3+ threes for the shooters: 48.9% /
49.0%; soft third 53.8% / 52.8%. This season's 24 nights say the same within their noise (25+ points for a star in a soft
third: said 50.8%, landed 45.4%).

So the 40-point night from a 26 projection is the top of a range the board already states: a star projected 24 scores
30+ three nights in ten and 35+ about one in seven, whatever the matchup. The matchup moves that by a few points of
chance (below), not by a tier.

## What the matchup and an absence are really worth (box scores, 2024-25 and 2025-26)

Stars (22+ points a game, 30+ minutes), their points against their own season average and how often they scored 30+:

| | landed / his average | 30+ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| opponent 1–5 in points allowed | 1.02 | 35% |
| 6–10 | 0.97 | 32% |
| 11–20 | 0.98 | 32% |
| 21–25 | 0.97 | 31% |
| 26–30 (the five tightest) | 0.92 | 26% |
| nobody out on his team | 0.90 | 25% |
| a 12–18 points teammate out | 1.00 | 36% |
| a 24+ points teammate out | 1.04 | 39% |

The five softest defenses are worth about + 3% and + 4 points of chance at 30+, the five tightest − 7%. A star teammate
out is the bigger lever (+ 14% between nobody out and a 24-point scorer out), and the board carries it: its lifted stars
ran 1 point high, not low. Rebounds and assists read the same way (tables).

## What it takes: the thresholds

His own volume does nearly all of the work. The widest rule that landed each target on 2024-25, with what it landed
after, unchanged:

| Mark | 50% | 60% | 70% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **3+ threes** | 5.9+ attempts and 1.9+ made a game over his last ten (50 → 47 → 52%; 26 players a night) | 7.4+ attempts and 2.8+ made (61 → 56 → 66%; 10 a night) | 10.3+ attempts and 3.5+ made (71 → 68%; 1–2 a night) |
| 2+ threes | 2.4+ attempts | 4.1+ attempts, 1.1+ made | 5.9+ attempts (70 → 69 → 72%) |
| 4+ threes | 10.3+ attempts (52 → 53%) | nobody | nobody |
| 20+ points | 11.7+ shots a game | 18+ points a game | 20.7+ points on 15.9+ shots (70 → 71 → 73%) |
| 25+ points | 16.9+ shots, 25+ in 3 of his last ten | 26.3+ points a game (60 → 64 → 66%) | 29.3+ points a game |
| 30+ points | 29.3+ a game, 19+ shots, 30+ in 4 of his last ten | nobody | nobody |
| 8+ rebounds | 6.1+ a game | 7.1+ a game | 8.1+ a game, 24+ minutes (71 → 65 → 73%) |
| 10+ rebounds | 7.5+ a game, 10+ in 3 of ten | 10+ in 5 of ten | 9.7+ a game, 10+ in 7 of ten |
| 6+ assists | 4.2+ a game, 6+ in 3 of ten | 5.3+ a game, 6+ in 4 of ten | 6+ in 7 of ten (72 → 69 → 67%) |
| 8+ assists | 6.7+ a game, 8+ in 4 of ten | 7.5+ a game, 8+ in 5 of ten | 9.2+ a game |
| 2+ steals | 1.8+ a game, 32+ minutes, 2+ in 5 of ten (51 → 48%) | 2.2+ a game, 32+ minutes | nobody |

**For 3+ threes the single best number is his three-point attempts a game over his last ten**: under 3.4 → 6%, 4.8–5.7 →
31%, 5.7–7 → 39%, 7–8.2 → 50%, 8.2–9.5 → 60%, 9.5+ → 68%. It held on every season.

**The PropFinder side adds a few points, no more.** Among the players for whom 3+ threes is a real question (1.8+ made a
game, 24+ minutes; 43% hit), by the opponent's rank in threes attempted against it: 1–5 → 44%, 6–10 → 46%, 11–20 → 44%,
21–25 → 44%, 26–30 → 38% (2024-25; 45 / 46 / 41 / 38 / 39% in 2025-26). The position cell reads no better than all
positions; the last ten no better than the season. A top-ten opponent lets a rule in at a slightly lower volume (2.4 made
a game in place of 2.8 for 60%), and that rule fell to 54% the next season where the plain one held at 56%. A 15-point
teammate out: 46% against 40% in 2024-25, 41% against 42% in 2025-26 — nothing dependable for threes. Starting matters
(45% against 36% off the bench), and the board reads it.

**The board already says these chances.** By his attempts over his last ten, 3+ threes over 2025-26: 4–6 → said 26.7%,
landed 27.2%; 6–8 → 43.7 / 43.8%; 8–10 → 56.4 / 55.4%; 10+ → 67.4 / 65.6%.

## Where the board is off, and what was tried

Each part the board multiplies in, read on top of the projection (0 = right, −0.5 = should count half):

| | Points | Rebounds | Assists | Threes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| the team ceiling's cut, 2025-26 | −0.54 | −0.67 | −0.44 | −0.55 |
| … this season | −0.28 | −0.52 | −0.32 | −1.04 |
| the matchup factor, 2025-26 | −0.11 | −0.08 | **−0.48** | −0.38 |
| a top-ten cell at his position (the ceiling read), 2025-26 | −0.03 | −0.02 | −0.03 | −0.05 |
| the teammates-out factor, 2025-26 | −0.27 | +0.07 | −0.20 | −0.35 |
| … this season | +0.45 | **+3.31** | +0.52 | **+2.38** |

1. **The team ceiling cuts too deep when it cuts deep.** A 20+ scorer whose side was cut 8%+ ran 1.88 points low over
   2025-26 (the one place the board is conservative with a star; 28% over his ceiling) and 0.73 low this season. But the
   sides it does not cut run 0.7–0.8 high both seasons, so loosening it does not pay: capped at 10% (`ceilingFloor` 0.9)
   points went 4.862 → 4.857 over 2025-26 and 4.783 → 4.804 this season; at half strength 4.872 and 4.829; without it
   this season 4.941 with the whole board 1.3 points high. Left as it is.
2. **The ceiling read (the lift toward his ceiling in a soft spot, 2026-10-31) costs a little everywhere.** Off (`reach`
   0), every stat reads better on both seasons: points 4.862 → 4.844 and 4.783 → 4.766, rebounds 1.958 → 1.951 and 1.912 →
   1.908, assists 1.442 → 1.436 and 1.412 → 1.408, threes 1.012 → 1.006 and 0.988 → 0.986, PRA 6.239 → 6.220 and 6.178 →
   6.165; the big players in a soft third from +0.84 high to +0.21. It is the opposite of "less conservative", and it was
   Vincent's ask, so it is **not changed here: his call**.
3. **The assists' matchup is about twice too strong.** Its exponents halved: 1.442 → 1.439 and 1.412 → 1.410. Too small
   to ship alone.
4. **Teammates out, rebounds and threes, this season only.** This season's lifted rows land above the board for rebounds
   (0.52 low at a 3%+ lift) and threes; over 2025-26 they landed on it. The exponents back up (0.6 / 0.7 for 0.3 / 0.4):
   this season's rebounds level (1.912), the lifted rows 1.978 → 1.959; 2025-26 worse (1.958 → 1.967). 24 nights against a
   season: not changed, to read again in a month.

No setting changed. No variant that pushes the big players up reads better on both seasons. What did improve the board came the same day from a different direction: a learned read of the teammates out and back and tonight's role, blended in (`2026-11-13-nba-model.md`).

## Shipped

- **Projected · Floor · Ceiling for every player** (the Games tab and the five stat lists): Floor and Ceiling are columns
  now (they sat in the projection's hover, which a phone does not have), and a player without a ladder for the stat is
  listed — 0 with a 0–0 range when his logs carry none (a centre's threes), "—" and "no games yet" when the pull has no
  game of his (`withEveryone` in `lib/picks/boards.js`).
- The replay writes the site's chance at round marks (`nba-audit.mjs --marks`) and each row's teammates-out factor, who is
  out and the opponent's all-positions rank; `ceilingPower` is a replay knob (1 on the site).

## Open, for Vincent

- The floor and the ceiling are the quartiles (he lands over his ceiling one night in four; measured 17–21%). If "ceiling"
  should mean what he does on a good night — one in ten — that is a different column, not a different model.
